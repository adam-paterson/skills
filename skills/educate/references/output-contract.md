# Teaching package output contract

Produce the parts the user needs now. For a multi-session programme, complete this contract for one session at a time rather than thinning every session into an outline. A full teaching package contains:

1. Teaching brief
2. Outcomes and evidence
3. Programme plan
4. Presentation manuscript
5. Learning and delivery materials
6. Assessment and transfer
7. Facilitation notes
8. Source notes

## 1. Teaching brief

State:

- audience and current capability
- motivation and real application context
- delivery constraints and important assumptions
- teaching mission
- explicit exclusions

## 2. Outcomes and evidence

Use a table:

| Learners will be able to… | Evidence during teaching | Evidence of later transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |

Use observable verbs. Replace “understand,” “learn about,” and “be aware of” with what learners will do to demonstrate the learning.

## 3. Programme plan

Use a table:

| Block | Time | Learner change | Teaching method | Practice and evidence | Optional? |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Show breaks and transitions. Total the timings. Mark the first material to remove if time runs short.

## 4. Presentation manuscript

Begin with the presentation's central claim, opening move, throughline, close, and Q&A bridge. Then use one row per slide:

| # | Time | Slide purpose | Exact on-slide copy | Visual or build direction | Speaker notes and transition | Sources |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

The manuscript must let the user build and deliver the presentation without reconstructing its missing argument:

- Make each title a claim, question, problem, or instruction rather than a generic topic label.
- Write the exact words that appear on screen. Keep them to one idea and usually no more than three short points.
- Give concrete visual direction: composition, labels, data, reveal order, artifact, demonstration, or state that no visual is needed.
- Write delivery-ready notes containing the explanation, example or story, audience question and anticipated response where useful, caveat, and transition to the next beat.
- Give the opening and close speakable wording. Use a full script for high-stakes or tightly timed passages; use structured notes elsewhere unless the user requests a verbatim script.
- Put stage, build, and interaction cues in brackets.
- Cite sourced claims in the row where they are used and provide full details in Source notes.
- End each section with retrieval, practice, a decision, or observable evidence.

A slide title plus a topic summary is a placeholder, not a completed slide manuscript.

## 5. Learning and delivery materials

List the required artifacts, then include their complete content. Create only artifacts used by the programme plan.

| Artifact | Used by | Purpose | Format or delivery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Make each named artifact ready to copy, print, rehearse, or build:

- **Audience handout or reference:** final headings, explanations, examples, citations, and next action.
- **Worksheet or exercise:** learner-facing instructions, prompts, inputs, constraints, and space or structure for the response.
- **Case or scenario:** complete situation, relevant facts or data, decision prompt, and staged reveals where needed.
- **Demonstration:** setup, exact sequence, narration cues, expected result, failure signals, and fallback.
- **Activity:** purpose, materials, grouping, exact instruction, timebox, instructor role, evidence to observe, feedback, and debrief questions.
- **Answer key or rubric:** acceptable response, reasoning, common errors, partial-performance signals, and feedback guidance.

For a talk without learner exercises, replace worksheets with the useful leave-behind: a one-page summary, checklist, reading path, or action guide. Naming an artifact without supplying its content does not satisfy this contract.

## 6. Assessment and transfer

Include:

- an initial diagnostic when placement is uncertain
- formative checks during teaching
- a final authentic performance or decision
- retrieval or spaced follow-up after the session
- a changed-context task that tests transfer
- answer criteria or a rubric for every assessed task

Prefer learner artifacts, observed performance, explanations, decisions, and error recovery over confidence ratings.

## 7. Facilitation notes

Include:

- preparation and materials checklist
- room, technology, accessibility, and environment validation
- likely misconceptions and concise responses
- pacing signals and material to cut or extend
- fallback plans for demonstrations or technology
- questions to collect evidence after delivery
- recommended updates to the stateful teaching workspace

## 8. Source notes

For every source used, provide the full citation or link, the claim or material it supports, its limits, and any freshness check still required. Distinguish sourced fact from practitioner judgment and illustrative examples created for teaching.
